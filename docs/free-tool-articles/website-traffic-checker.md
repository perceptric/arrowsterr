## What is a website traffic checker?

A website traffic checker estimates how many visits a website gets. This one estimates monthly visits from Google organic search for any domain, in the country you choose. It also shows what that traffic would cost in Google Ads, how many keywords bring it in and which pages earn the most.

The key word is estimate. Only the site owner can see a site's real visit count, in tools like Google Analytics. Everyone else has to work from public signals. For search traffic, the best public signal is where a site ranks in Google and how many people search those keywords.

Visits to a website come from several channels. Here are the common ones:

- **Organic search:** people who click a regular Google or Bing result.
- **Paid search:** people who click an ad.
- **Direct:** people who type the address or use a bookmark.
- **Referral:** people who click a link on another site.
- **Social and email:** people who click a post or a newsletter link.

This tool measures the first one, and only for Google. It does not see ads, direct visits, referrals, social or email. That matters when you read the result. A brand with a huge newsletter and a weak blog will look small here, even if its site is busy.

So why check Google organic traffic at all? Because it is the channel you can study from the outside. It tells you how much demand a site captures from search, and which topics bring it. Use it to:

- Size up a competitor's search channel before you invest in yours.
- Check a site before you buy a link, a sponsorship or the whole business.
- See which pages a rival's search traffic depends on.
- Get a quick outside view of your own site to compare with your analytics.

## Where the numbers come from

The data comes from DataForSEO Labs. DataForSEO sells search data that many SEO tools are built on. Labs is its database of Google rankings. It runs a very large set of keywords through Google, in each country, and records the pages that rank. It refreshes monthly.

### How the visit estimate is built

No outside tool can see inside a website's analytics. So the visit number is built from the outside in:

1. Find every keyword in the database where the domain ranks in the top 100.
2. Take the monthly search volume for each keyword.
3. Multiply by the share of clicks a result usually gets at that position.
4. Add up the results across all keywords.

A page at #1 for a keyword with 10,000 searches gets a big slice of those clicks. A page at #40 gets close to none. The total across all keywords is the monthly estimate.

### How traffic value is built

Traffic value uses the same keywords. For each one, it takes the estimated visits and multiplies them by the keyword's cost per click in Google Ads. Then it adds them up.

The result answers one question: if this site had to buy the same visits with ads, what would it pay each month? It is a way to weigh traffic by how much advertisers pay for it. 1,000 visits from "best payroll software" are worth far more to a business than 1,000 visits from "funny cat names".

### How close the estimate gets

Expect the estimate to be off, sometimes by a lot. The reasons are worth knowing:

- **No database has every keyword.** Rare and very specific searches often fall outside it. Sites that get many small long-tail visits can look smaller than they are.
- **Volumes are averages.** Google Ads data groups many volumes into ranges. Seasonal keywords swing far above and below their average.
- **Click shares vary by page.** An AI Overview, ads, a map pack or a People also ask box can push organic results down and take clicks.
- **Brand searches are hard to model.** When people search a brand by name, almost all of them click it. Big brands can be under-counted.
- **One country, one moment.** Rankings move by city and device, and they move day to day. A monthly database captures one view.

Estimates are most useful as comparisons. Two sites checked the same way, in the same country, in the same month, are fair to compare. The same site checked month after month shows a real direction.

### Website traffic checker vs Google Analytics

People often run this tool on their own site, then wonder why it doesn't match Google Analytics. They measure different things in different ways.

| | Website Traffic Checker | Google Search Console | Google Analytics |

| Who can use it | Anyone, on any domain | Site owner only | Site owner only |
| What it counts | Estimated Google organic visits | Real clicks from Google Search | Real sessions from every channel |
| How | Rankings times volumes times click shares | Google's own logs | A tracking script on your pages |
| Channels | Google organic only | Google Search only | Organic, paid, direct, referral, social, email and more |

## How to read your results

Each part of the result answers a different question.

**Estimated monthly visits.** How many visits Google organic search sends the domain in a month, for the chosen country. It is the latest month in the data.

**Traffic value.** What those visits would cost to buy as Google Ads clicks, in US dollars. It tells you how commercial the traffic is. It is not revenue, and the site does not earn this money.

**Ranking keywords.** How many keywords the domain ranks for in the top 100. It shows how wide the site's search footprint is. Most of these keywords bring very few visits.

**12-month trend.** Estimated visits for each month of the past year. This is the part most people skip, and it is often the most useful. It shows direction and seasons.

**Top 5 pages.** The pages with the most estimated visits, each with its keyword count. Pages with many visits from few keywords depend on 1 or 2 big terms. Pages with visits spread across hundreds of keywords are steadier.

One handy number is not on screen, and you can work it out: traffic value divided by visits. That gives you the average ad value of one visit. A high number means the site ranks for keywords advertisers fight over.

The table below gives rough ranges. Treat them as a starting point. They swing a lot by niche. A B2B software site and a recipe blog should never be judged on the same scale, so compare against sites like the one you checked.

| Signal | Good | Average | Weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12-month trend | Up over most of the year | Flat, or a clear seasonal pattern | Down 3 months in a row or more |
| Share of visits from the top page | Under 30% | 30% to 50% | Over 50% |
| Top page's keyword count | Hundreds of keywords | Dozens | Fewer than 10 |
| Value per visit (B2B or finance) | Above the niche's typical CPC | Near it | Far below it |
| Value per visit (content or media) | Any steady value | Low but rising | Near $0 and falling |

## What to do with your results

Here are 7 ways to use a traffic check, each with a clear next step.

### Size up a competitor before you commit

Before you spend months on SEO in a new market, check how much search traffic the leaders get there. Run 3 or 4 rivals in the same country.

If the biggest player gets a few thousand visits a month, search is a small channel in that market. If several rivals get hundreds of thousands, search is where buyers look. That helps you decide how much to put into content and links. For the keywords behind their traffic, run the [Keyword Gap tool](/free-tools/keyword-gap/) with your domain and theirs.

### Copy the shape of their best pages

The top 5 pages tell you what a rival's search traffic is made of. Is it product pages? A glossary? Free tools? Comparison posts?

Open each page and note its format and the problem it solves. If 3 of a rival's top 5 pages are free calculators, searchers in your market want quick answers they can use. Plan pages that solve the same problems better. Don't copy their words. Match the need.

### Vet a site before you buy a link or a sponsorship

Many sites sell guest posts, links or ad space. Some have strong numbers on a link metric and almost no real visitors. That often means the site exists to sell links.

Run the site through the traffic checker. A site with steady organic traffic on topics close to yours is a fair partner. A site with near-zero traffic, or a trend that crashed, is a poor buy. Check its link profile too with the [Backlink Checker](/free-tools/backlink-checker/) and its authority with the [Domain Authority Checker](/free-tools/domain-authority-checker/).

### Use traffic value to make the case for SEO

Traffic value turns rankings into a number a finance team understands. If your organic traffic is worth $20,000 a month in ad spend, that is money you would have to pay Google to replace.

Run your own domain and a rival's. Show the gap in traffic value. It is a simple way to explain why content and links deserve a budget. Be clear that it is an ad-cost estimate, and keep revenue out of it.

### Plan around the seasons you can see

The 12-month chart often shows seasons. Tax software peaks in spring in the United States. Gift guides peak before the holidays.

If a rival's traffic climbs every year in the same month, the demand comes back on a schedule. Publish or update your pages 2 or 3 months before the peak, so Google has time to find and rank them. Check the seasonal pattern of single keywords with the [Keyword Volume Checker](/free-tools/keyword-volume-checker/).

### Calibrate the estimate against your own data

Run the tool on your own domain. Then open Search Console and look at clicks for the same country over the same month.

If the estimate is half your real clicks, the model is missing many of your keywords. Keep that ratio in mind when you look at rivals in the same niche. It turns a rough number into a better one for your market.

### Turn their top pages into your keyword list

Each top page ranks for many keywords. The page with the most visits often ranks for hundreds of related searches.

Open Website Research in the [app](https://app.arrowsterr.com/) to see the full keyword list for that page. Feed the main ones into the [Free Keyword Tool](/free-tools/keyword-tool/) to see volume and difficulty. You now have a tested list of topics that already bring traffic in your market.

## Common mistakes

Most wrong conclusions come from one of these.

### Reading the number as total site traffic

The tool counts Google organic visits only. A brand with a big email list or heavy ad spend gets far more visits than this shows.

**Fix:** Say "Google organic visits" whenever you share the number. Never compare it with a total-traffic figure from another source.

### Expecting it to match Google Analytics

Analytics counts sessions from every channel with a script on the site. This tool models clicks from rankings.

**Fix:** Compare the estimate with Search Console clicks for the same month and country. That is the closest like-for-like check.

### Treating traffic value as income

Traffic value is what the visits would cost in ads. A blog with high traffic value may earn very little from those visitors.

**Fix:** Use traffic value to compare how commercial two sites' traffic is. Use real sales data for revenue.

### Trusting tiny numbers

For small sites, a few missing keywords can be most of the traffic. A site may show 0 visits and still get real search clicks.

**Fix:** For sites with very small estimates, treat the result as "low" and leave it there. Look at the ranking keywords to see what the site does show up for.

### Mixing countries

A US check and a UK check are two different sites as far as search data goes.

**Fix:** Check every site in the same country. For a global view, run each main market and add them up yourself.

### Judging by visits without checking the keywords

A site can get lots of visits for keywords that have nothing to do with its business. A software company might rank for a viral meme. That traffic rarely becomes customers.

**Fix:** Look at the top pages and ask whether those visitors could buy. Weigh traffic by fit.

## Glossary

**Organic traffic:** Visits that come from clicking a regular search result. Nobody pays per click for these.

**Paid traffic:** Visits from clicking an ad, such as a Google Ads result.

**Direct traffic:** Visits where someone typed the address or used a bookmark. Analytics also files visits it can't trace here.

**Referral traffic:** Visits from clicking a link on another website.

**Session:** One visit in Google Analytics. A session can include many page views.

**Traffic value:** The estimated cost of a site's organic visits if it bought them as Google Ads clicks.

**Cost per click (CPC):** What advertisers pay on average for one click on an ad for a keyword.

**Ranking keyword:** A search where the site appears in Google's top 100 results.

**Search Console:** Google's free tool for site owners. It shows real clicks, impressions and average positions from Google Search.

**Seasonality:** A regular rise and fall in searches at the same time each year.

**Click share:** The portion of searchers who click a result at a given position.

## The AI search angle

Search traffic is changing shape. More questions now get answered by AI before anyone clicks. That affects how you should read any traffic estimate.

**Google AI Overviews.** For many searches, Google shows an AI-written answer at the top, with a few cited links. When the answer is enough, fewer people scroll to the regular results. A site can hold its rankings and still see fewer visits. If a rival's trend falls while its rankings hold, AI Overviews may be the reason. Check a keyword with the [AI Overview Checker](/free-tools/ai-overview-checker/) to see if Google shows one and which sites it cites.

**Google AI Mode.** AI Mode gives a longer, chat-style answer with its own list of sources. A site cited there can get visits even without a top organic spot. The [Keyword Rank Checker](/free-tools/keyword-rank-checker/) shows your Google position and your AI Mode citation for the same keyword.

**ChatGPT.** Many people now ask ChatGPT what to buy or which tool to use. Visits from ChatGPT links usually appear in Google Analytics as referrals. A traffic checker built on Google rankings will never see them. ChatGPT may also name your brand without linking at all. The [ChatGPT Rank Checker](/free-tools/chatgpt-rank-checker/) tells you whether you get named for a buyer question.

So a Google traffic estimate is now one half of a site's search reach. The other half is how often AI answers mention it. To be cited at all, AI crawlers have to be able to read your pages. The [AI Crawler Checker](/free-tools/ai-crawler-checker/) shows whether your robots.txt or firewall blocks them.

## FAQ

### How can I check how much traffic a website gets?

Enter the domain and a country in the Website Traffic Checker. It estimates monthly visits from Google organic search, using DataForSEO's ranking data. For your own site, Google Analytics and Search Console show real numbers.

### Can I see the exact traffic of another website?

No outside tool can. Only the site owner sees real visit counts. Tools like this one estimate search traffic from rankings and search volumes.

### How accurate are website traffic checkers?

They are models, so expect gaps. Estimates work well for comparing sites checked the same way and for spotting trends. They are weak as exact counts, especially for small sites.

### Does this include direct, social or paid traffic?

No. It estimates Google organic search visits only. Ads, direct visits, referrals, social and email are not included.

### Why is the estimate different from my Google Analytics?

Analytics counts real sessions from every channel. This tool models Google organic clicks from rankings. Compare it with Search Console clicks for the same month and country for a closer match.

### What is traffic value?

Traffic value is what a site's organic visits would cost if bought as Google Ads clicks. It is worked out from each keyword's estimated visits and its cost per click. It shows how commercial the traffic is. The site does not earn this money.

### Why does a website show 0 traffic?

The site may rank for keywords outside the database, or only rank on deep pages of results. New and very small sites often show 0. Check a different country too, in case its traffic comes from elsewhere.

### Is the Website Traffic Checker free?

Yes. You get 5 checks a day with no sign-up. Results are saved for 30 days, and checking the same domain and country again in that time is free.

### How often is the traffic data updated?

The data comes from DataForSEO Labs, which updates monthly. Changes from the last few weeks may not show yet.

### Can I check traffic for a single page?

The free tool shows the top 5 pages for a domain with their visits and keyword counts. Website Research in the Arrowsterr app lists every page.

### Can I check traffic in different countries?

Yes. Pick a country before you run the check. Each country is a separate check, so a global site needs one run per market.

### How much traffic does an average website get?

There is no useful average. It ranges from almost none for a new local site to many millions for large publishers. Compare a site with direct rivals in the same country instead.

### Can website traffic be faked in this tool?

The estimate comes from Google rankings, so bot visits to a site do not change it. A site can still rank for off-topic keywords that bring useless traffic. Check the top pages to see what the visits are for.

### What's the difference between this and the Domain Overview?

Both use the same data. The Website Traffic Checker focuses on visits, traffic value and pages. The Domain Overview adds ranking spread, top keywords and keywords gained and lost.
