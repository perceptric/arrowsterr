# AirOps vs Profound: AI Search Tracking and Content Tools Compared in 2026

AirOps and Profound both help brands show up in AI answers. They start from different ends. AirOps puts content work at the center: workflows that research, write, review and refresh pages, with AI search tracking on top. Profound puts AI search data at the center. It covers up to 9 answer engines with prompt volumes and AI crawler data, and its AI Marketer drafts work for you to approve.

Here is the quick answer. Pick AirOps if your team ships a lot of content and wants workflows and human review in one place. Pick Profound if you are a large brand that needs deep AI search data with SSO and SOC 2, and you are fine with a sales-led contract. Neither vendor shows a dollar price on its pricing page, so plan on a call with either one.

## AirOps vs Profound: main points

- **Focus.** AirOps is a content platform with AI search insights. Profound is an AI search platform with content agents.
- **AI engines.** AirOps Pro lists insights across "10+ answer engines." Profound Enterprise covers up to 9, including DeepSeek and Exa Search.
- **Content tools.** AirOps has deeper content tools: tasks, Brand Kits, Knowledge Bases, Grids, playbooks and human review steps.
- **AI crawler data.** Profound's Agent Analytics connects to your CDN or host and is included on the trial and Enterprise.
- **Trial.** Profound has a free 7-day trial with fixed prompts. AirOps shows "Start for free" on its Solo and Pro plans.
- **Price.** Neither lists a dollar price. Profound's ongoing use is Enterprise only. AirOps sells Solo, Pro and Enterprise.

### What AirOps does today

[AirOps](https://www.airops.com/) calls itself "the partner behind your brand's AI search strategy." It sells a platform plus expert help. The platform runs content workflows that use AI agents to research, create, publish, review and refresh pages. It also tracks how AI answers name your brand, through a part of the product called Insights. AirOps names Ramp, Webflow, Kayak, Klaviyo and Chime among the teams that use it.

AirOps bills by tasks. A task is one action in a workflow, like generating content or pulling data. Solo includes 35,000 tasks and Pro includes 100,000. AirOps also offers strategy services from its own team.

### AirOps history

Alex Halliday, Berna Gonzalez and Matt Hammel founded AirOps. Before that, the three built products at MasterClass and Bungalow. The company started out in Miami. Its first product, AirOps Apps, went into private beta in late 2022. Apps let teams drop task-based AI tools into the work they already do, from writing content to writing SQL queries.

In April 2023, AirOps raised a $7 million seed round led by Wing VC. A $15.5 million Series A led by Unusual Ventures followed in October 2024. By then, AirOps had made content marketing its main focus.

In November 2025, AirOps raised a $40 million Series B led by Greylock. That brought its total funding to $60 million. Along with the round, it launched what it calls "the first end-to-end content engineering platform built to win AI search." The launch added Insights for AI visibility across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews. It also added Page360, one view of each page's SEO and AI search data. AirOps Actions then turns those findings into on-brand content.

### What Profound does today

[Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/) calls itself "the AI marketing platform." It tracks how AI answers describe your category and brand across up to 9 engines. It adds Prompt Volumes, which shows what people ask AI. ChatGPT Shopping data and Agent Analytics for AI bots on your site come with it too.

Its AI Marketer finds work worth doing, drafts it and asks you to approve it. That covers pages, briefs, social posts and corrections to wrong claims about your brand. Profound sells ongoing use as Enterprise only, after a 7-day free trial. In September 2026, it said more than 2,500 brands use it, including Walmart, Zoom, Stripe and Figma.

### Profound history

James Cadwallader and Dylan Babbs started Profound in 2024. They met at South Park Commons, a startup community and early-stage fund. Cadwallader had founded the influencer marketing firm Kyra. Babbs was a software engineer at Uber.

Funding came fast. Profound closed a $3.5 million seed round in August 2024, with Khosla Ventures and South Park Commons among its backers. A $20 million Series A led by Kleiner Perkins followed in June 2025. That same month, Profound earned SOC 2 Type 2. In August 2025, Sequoia led a $35 million Series B.

In February 2026, a $96 million Series C led by Lightspeed Venture Partners valued Profound at $1 billion. Fortune reported more than 700 enterprise customers at the time, along with the launch of Profound Agents, which write marketing copy in a brand's voice. An Agent Template Marketplace came in June 2026.

In September 2026, Profound raised a $180 million Series D at a $1.8 billion valuation, co-led by Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins. Its Series D post puts AI Marketer, also called Aim, at the heart of the product. A new generation of Agents followed in October 2026.

AirOps is stronger here. Content work is what it was built around.

AirOps gives you tasks to run workflows. Brand Kits hold your voice and style, and Knowledge Bases hold facts about your company. Its pricing page also lists an inbox for content work.

Profound's AI Marketer looks at your AI search data, picks the work worth doing and drafts it for you to approve. Its home page names pages built for AI answers and briefs turned into content and channel cutdowns. It also drafts social posts with images sized per platform. Fixes for wrong claims about your brand come traced to their source. Launch kits pair your messaging with one-pagers and FAQs. A Context Manager keeps your company and competitor facts up to date, along with your audience.

Pick AirOps if you want to design your own content system and run it at volume. Pick Profound if you want an assistant that turns AI search data into drafts for you.

## Which should you choose: AirOps or Profound?

**Pick AirOps if:**

- Your team publishes and refreshes a lot of content.
- You want to build your own workflows with brand rules and review steps.
- You want unlimited seats on a plan below Enterprise.
- AI search tracking is a check on your content work, and you don't need every engine.

**Pick Profound if:**

- AI search data is the main job, and you need up to 9 engines.
- You need DeepSeek, Exa Search or ChatGPT Shopping data.
- You want AI crawler data tied to your CDN or host.
- You need SSO, SOC 2 and a support SLA on a custom contract.

## AirOps vs Profound FAQ

### Is AirOps cheaper than Profound?

You can't tell from their sites. Neither AirOps nor Profound shows a dollar price on its pricing page. AirOps sells Solo and Pro below Enterprise, while Profound sells ongoing use as Enterprise only. Ask both for a quote on the same prompt count and engine list.

### Does AirOps track AI search visibility?

Yes. AirOps Insights tracks how AI answers name your brand. Solo covers ChatGPT only. Pro covers 10+ answer engines, and the docs name ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, AI Mode, AI Overviews, Claude, Copilot, Grok and Muse Spark.

### Can Profound write content?

Yes. Profound's AI Marketer drafts pages, briefs, social posts and launch kits, then asks you to approve them. It also drafts fixes for wrong claims about your brand. AirOps gives you more control over how content workflows are built and reviewed.

### Does Profound have a free plan?

No. Profound has a free 7-day trial with 50 fixed prompts on ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. After that, you need an Enterprise contract or the Agency Growth plan.

### Which tool tracks more AI engines?

It depends on the engines you need. Profound Enterprise covers 9, including DeepSeek and Exa Search. AirOps Pro lists 10+ answer engines, with Grok and Muse Spark named in its docs. Match the list to the AI tools your buyers use.
